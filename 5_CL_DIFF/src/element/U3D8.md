# Deep Dive: The `u3d8.f90` Core Engine

The `u3d8.f90` file is the mathematical heart of the finite element formulation. It is responsible for bridging the discrete nodal degrees of freedom (displacements and chemical potentials) with the continuous physical equations computed in your material subroutine.

This document breaks down the exact chronological sequence of operations performed during a single execution of this subroutine.

---

## 1. Initialization and Nodal Extraction
Before any complex math begins, the element prepares its memory:
1.  **Zeroing Arrays:** The local residual vectors ($\mathbf{R}_u, \mathbf{R}_c$) and partitioned tangent stiffness matrices ($\mathbf{K}_{uu}, \mathbf{K}_{cc}, \mathbf{K}_{uc}, \mathbf{K}_{cu}$) are initialized to zero.
2.  **State Extraction:** The subroutine pulls the known degrees of freedom from the Abaqus global arrays. For every node $k$, it extracts:
    *   The displacement increment $\Delta \mathbf{u}^k$ and current displacement $\mathbf{u}^k$.
    *   The chemical potential increment $\Delta \mu^k$ and current potential $\mu^k$.
3.  **Coordinate Update:** The current spatial coordinates $\mathbf{x}^k$ are computed by adding the displacement to the reference coordinates $\mathbf{X}^k$:
    $$ \mathbf{x}^k = \mathbf{X}^k + \mathbf{u}^k $$

## 2. The F-Bar Method Setup (Volumetric Locking Prevention)
Because you are modeling a nearly incompressible polymer network, standard 8-node brick elements are highly prone to **volumetric locking** (where the element becomes artificially stiff and the solver crashes). To prevent this without requiring a mixed u-p element formulation, this code employs the **F-Bar ($\bar{F}$) Method**.

The core idea of the F-Bar method is to enforce a constant volumetric strain across the entire element, rather than letting it vary point-by-point.
1.  **Centroid Evaluation:** The routine evaluates the shape functions $\mathbf{N}$ and their derivatives exactly at the **centroid** of the element ($\xi = \eta = \zeta = 0$).
2.  **Centroidal Kinematics:** It computes the deformation gradient strictly at the centroid, $\mathbf{F}_c$:
    $$ \mathbf{F}_c = \sum_{k=1}^8 \mathbf{x}^k \otimes \nabla_{\mathbf{X}} N^k_c $$
3.  **Volumetric Capture:** It calculates the volume change at the centroid, $J_c = \det(\mathbf{F}_c)$, for both the start and end of the increment.

## 3. The Integration Point Loop (Gauss Quadrature)
The subroutine opens a loop over the 8 volume integration points (`DO intpt = 1, nInttPt`). Everything inside this loop is evaluated at a specific mathematical point $(\xi_p, \eta_p, \zeta_p)$.

### 3a. Kinematics and Gradients
At the current integration point, the element computes the local physics:
*   **Chemical Gradients:** It calculates the local chemical potential $\mu$ and its true spatial gradient $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}} \mu$:
    $$ \mu = \sum_{k=1}^8 N^k \mu^k, \qquad \nabla_{\mathbf{x}} \mu = \sum_{k=1}^8 \mu^k \nabla_{\mathbf{x}} N^k $$
*   **The F-Bar Modified Deformation Gradient:** It calculates the standard deformation gradient $\mathbf{F}$ at the integration point, but then **modifies** it by stripping out its local volume change $\det(\mathbf{F})$ and replacing it with the centroid's volume change $J_c$:
    $$ \bar{\mathbf{F}} = \left( \frac{J_c}{\det(\mathbf{F})} \right)^{1/3} \mathbf{F} $$
    This modified $\bar{\mathbf{F}}$ is what gets sent to the material subroutine, ensuring the element doesn't lock!

### 3b. The Constitutive Call
The subroutine calls your `MATERIAL` subroutine (`_umat_.f90`), passing in $\bar{\mathbf{F}}$, $\mu$, and the time increment $\Delta t$. 

The material subroutine acts as a black box and returns:
*   The Cauchy stress tensor ($\mathbf{T}$).
*   The spatial tangent moduli ($\mathbb{C}$, or `SpTanMod`).
*   Chemical state updates (e.g., fluid concentration rate $\dot{c}$, flux $\mathbf{j}$, and chemical tangents).

## 4. Constructing the Residuals (The Weak Form)
With the physical stress and chemical state known, the element numerically integrates the "Weak Form" of the governing equations over the element volume $V$. 
Integration is done by multiplying the values by the Gauss weight $w_p$ and the volume Jacobian $dV = \det(\mathbf{J}) d\xi d\eta d\zeta$.

### The Mechanical Residual ($\mathbf{R}_u$)
This enforces the balance of linear momentum (equilibrium). The residual vector is constructed by integrating the stress $\mathbf{T}$ against the gradient of the shape functions (often called the $\mathbf{B}$-matrix):
$$ \mathbf{R}_u = \int_V \left( \nabla_{\mathbf{x}} \mathbf{N}^T \mathbf{T} - \mathbf{N}^T \mathbf{b} \right) dV \approx \sum_{p=1}^8 \left( \mathbf{B}^T \mathbf{T} \right) w_p \det(\mathbf{J})_p $$

### The Chemical Residual ($R_c$)
This enforces the conservation of mass (fluid/crosslinker diffusion). It integrates the concentration rate against the shape functions, and the spatial flux against the shape function gradient:
$$ R_c = \int_V \left( N \frac{\partial c}{\partial t} + \nabla_{\mathbf{x}} N \cdot \mathbf{j} \right) dV $$
*(Note: In the code, flux is driven by mobility $M$ and the potential gradient $\nabla \mu$)*

## 5. Constructing the Tangent Stiffness Matrices (The Jacobian)
To achieve quadratic convergence in the Newton-Raphson solver, Abaqus requires the exact derivative of the Residuals with respect to the nodal Degrees of Freedom. 

Because this is a coupled problem, a $4 \times 4$ block matrix must be assembled:
$$ \begin{bmatrix} \Delta \mathbf{R}_u \\ \Delta R_c \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{K}_{uu} & \mathbf{K}_{uc} \\ \mathbf{K}_{cu} & K_{cc} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \Delta \mathbf{u} \\ \Delta \mu \end{bmatrix} $$

### Mechanical Tangent ($\mathbf{K}_{uu}$)
This dictates how the mechanical residual changes when nodes are displaced. It combines the material stiffness ($\mathbb{C}$) and the geometric stiffness (due to large deformations):
$$ \mathbf{K}_{uu} = \int_V \left( \nabla_{\mathbf{x}} \mathbf{N}^T \mathbb{C} \nabla_{\mathbf{x}} \mathbf{N} + \text{Initial Stress Terms} \right) dV $$
*(The code also includes complex tensor corrections `Qmat` and `Gmat` specifically to make the tangent consistent with the F-Bar volumetric modification).*

### Chemical Tangent ($K_{cc}$)
This dictates how the chemical residual changes with respect to the chemical potential. It relies heavily on the fluid mobility $M$ and the derivative $\frac{\partial c}{\partial \mu}$:
$$ K_{cc} = \int_V \left( \mathbf{N}^T \left[ \frac{\partial \dot{c}}{\partial \mu} \right] \mathbf{N} + \nabla_{\mathbf{x}} \mathbf{N}^T M \nabla_{\mathbf{x}} \mathbf{N} \right) dV $$

### Coupling Tangents ($\mathbf{K}_{uc}$ and $\mathbf{K}_{cu}$)
These are the off-diagonal matrices that make the element "Coupled". 
*   $\mathbf{K}_{uc}$: Dictates how the mechanical stress changes when the chemical potential (swelling) increases. (Powered by `SpUCMod` $\approx \frac{\partial \mathbf{T}}{\partial \mu}$)
*   $\mathbf{K}_{cu}$: Dictates how the fluid concentration changes when the element is physically stretched. (Powered by `AmatCU`)

## 6. Surface Flux Integration (Boundary Conditions)
After the volume loop finishes, the element checks if the user applied any boundary fluxes (e.g., `*Dsload` in the input file).
If `ndload > 0`, it opens a new loop over the specified element faces. 
It calls the 2D surface quadrature routines (`xintSurf3D4pt`), evaluates the 2D surface area Jacobian ($dA$), and subtracts the external flux $q$ from the nodes attached to that face:
$$ \mathbf{R}_c = \mathbf{R}_c - \int_{\Gamma} \mathbf{N}^T q \, dA $$

## 7. Global Assembly
Finally, `u3d8.f90` takes all of its local sub-matrices ($\mathbf{R}_u, R_c, \mathbf{K}_{uu}, K_{cc}, \mathbf{K}_{uc}, \mathbf{K}_{cu}$) and calls `AssembleElement` to pack them perfectly into the monolithic `rhs` and `amatrx` arrays to be sent back to the Abaqus solver!
